The 12 Rules of Foraging.

Every harvest affects a living ecosystem. Learn the essentials of safe identification, responsible collecting, and respect for the land before gathering anything.

  1. 01

    Identify Before You Harvest

    Confirm an organism’s identity and learn its dangerous look-alikes.

  2. 02

    Know the Laws of the Land

    Check permissions, permits, land rules, and protected species.

  3. 03

    Take Only What You Need

    Harvest with purpose, and avoid unnecessary waste.

  4. 04

    Harvest Sustainably

    Leave enough for wildlife, reproduction, and regeneration.

  5. 05

    Respect Wildlife and Habitat

    Protect pollinators, nesting animals, soil life, and habitats.

  6. 06

    Protect Rare and Vulnerable Species

    Never collect protected species or from declining populations.

  7. 07

    Minimize Unnecessary Damage

    Avoid harming roots, mycelium, trees, and surrounding plants.

  8. 08

    Know Your Environment

    Avoid polluted sites, treated areas, and contaminated soil or water.

  9. 09

    Prevent Ecological Harm

    Avoid spreading invasive species, pests, and pathogens.

  10. 10

    Honor Indigenous Knowledge

    Respect living knowledge holders, land rights, and traditions.

  11. 11

    Practice Reciprocity

    Restore habitats, remove litter safely, and protect biodiversity.

  12. 12

    Know When Not to Harvest

    Sometimes observation is the most responsible choice.

Safety first: Never identify or eat wild plants or fungi using this app alone. Verify with expert-quality sources, check local restrictions, and avoid contaminated sites.

Why this knowledge matters

Plants dominate the living world around us. Yet research shows we routinely fail to notice them.

This isn't nostalgia. Plant literacy affects how we understand biodiversity, conservation, food, culture and the ecosystems keeping us alive.

SAFETY · STEWARDSHIP · RECIPROCITY

The Forager's Code.

Principles of Responsible & Ethical Foraging

Observe first. Harvest responsibly. Give back whenever possible.

Foraging is more than identifying edible plants and fungi. It is an opportunity to understand the living world, recognize our place within ecosystems, and practice responsible stewardship. Nature is not an unlimited resource. Every organism contributes to a larger ecological community.

SafetyKnow the organism, location and risks.
StewardshipProtect populations, habitats and regeneration.
ReciprocityRespect relationships and contribute to ecological health.
THE 12 PRINCIPLES

Take knowledge into the field.

A practical code for learning from nature without treating it as an unlimited resource.

01🔍Safety

Identify Before You Harvest

Never consume a wild plant or fungus without confidently verifying its identity using multiple distinguishing characteristics and reliable expert sources. Learn dangerous look-alikes. When uncertain, leave it untouched.

02⚖️Safety

Know the Laws of the Land

Research local regulations, protected species, harvesting restrictions and permit requirements. Obtain permission before entering private property or collecting where access is restricted.

03🌱Stewardship

Take Only What You Need

Harvest with purpose, not excess. Collect only what you can responsibly use, and avoid unnecessary waste.

04🌿Stewardship

Harvest Sustainably

Only harvest from healthy, abundant populations. Leave enough plants, fruiting bodies, seeds and reproductive structures for regeneration and other species. There is no universal safe harvesting percentage.

05🐝Reciprocity

Respect Wildlife and Habitat

Wild plants and fungi provide food, shelter and habitat for other organisms. Avoid disturbing nests, burrows, pollinators, soil communities and surrounding habitats.

06🌼Stewardship

Protect Rare and Vulnerable Species

Never harvest threatened, endangered, legally protected or locally declining species. Small populations may be especially vulnerable to disturbance.

07🍄Stewardship

Minimize Unnecessary Damage

Use appropriate harvesting methods for each species. Avoid damage to roots, mycelium, trees, nearby vegetation and soil. Do not uproot an organism when a less destructive method is suitable.

08🏙️Safety

Know Your Environment

Avoid collecting food from potentially contaminated roadsides, chemically treated lawns, industrial areas, polluted waterways and sites with unknown soil or water quality.

09🌍Stewardship

Prevent Ecological Harm

Clean footwear and equipment when necessary to reduce the spread of invasive species, pests and pathogens. Do not relocate wild organisms without understanding legal and ecological consequences.

10🪶Reciprocity

Honor Indigenous Knowledge

Recognize the longstanding ecological knowledge of Indigenous communities. Respect living knowledge holders, distinct cultural traditions, land rights and community-specific teachings.

11♻️Reciprocity

Practice Reciprocity

Look for meaningful ways to help ecosystems flourish. Safely remove litter, participate in habitat restoration, support native biodiversity and share accurate knowledge responsibly.

12💚Reciprocity

Know When Not to Harvest

Not every edible organism needs to become food. Sometimes observation without collection is the most responsible choice.

OUR GUIDING PHILOSOPHY

The goal is not to take as much as possible from nature, but to learn to live responsibly within it.

A successful forager leaves an ecosystem capable of continuing to thrive long after they have departed.

Learn the land. Respect its inhabitants. Protect its future.

Keep learning

These principles are general guidance, not a substitute for local regulations, species-specific conservation guidance, or qualified in-person identification. Avoid consuming any wild organism based on this app alone.

About Urban Forager

A free, mobile-first field reference for learning wild plants and fungi through identification, food, nutrition, documented traditional knowledge, history, modern research and safety.

Foraging safety

Never eat, drink, or medicinally use a wild plant or fungus based solely on this app. Positive identification requires multiple diagnostic features, and edible organisms can have dangerous or deadly look-alikes.

Every dossier now follows the same evidence rules. Traditional or historical use does not establish clinical effectiveness, missing nutrition data is not guessed, and herbs can interact with medicines.

RECIPROCITY

Stewardship, not extraction

Harvest only where permitted. Take only what you need. Protect populations and habitat, leave enough for wildlife and regeneration, and avoid creating waste while gathering or preparing food and medicine.

HOW CLAIMS ARE HANDLED

Accuracy & evidence policy

Urban Forager applies one evidence standard across all 174 current dossiers and future additions. Claims are retained only when supported, explicitly qualified, or clearly labeled as historical/traditional documentation.

  • Identification: verify names, diagnostic features, range and look-alikes against botanical authorities and qualified field references.
  • Nutrition: prefer quantitative food-composition data; unsupported qualitative rankings are removed rather than treated as verified.
  • Medicine: distinguish traditional use, preclinical research, human evidence and safety.
  • Indigenous uses: identify the specific nation/community and historical source rather than collapsing distinct peoples into a generic claim.
  • Uncertainty: gaps or conflicting evidence are stated rather than filled in.
