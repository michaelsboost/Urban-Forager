The world beneath your feet

You know your phone.
Do you know the land?

We move through a living world every day while barely knowing its names. Food, fiber, medicine, ecology, history and generations of human knowledge can be growing outside the door. Urban Forager exists to help rebuild that relationship.

There is a name for the disconnect

Plant Awareness Disparity

Researchers use this term for the tendency to overlook plants, know less about them and treat them as background rather than distinct living organisms.

Read the peer-reviewed research ↗

Why this knowledge matters

Plants dominate the living world around us. Yet research shows we routinely fail to notice them.

This isn't nostalgia. Plant literacy affects how we understand biodiversity, conservation, food, culture and the ecosystems keeping us alive.

SAFETY / STEWARDSHIP / EVIDENCE

About Urban Forager

A free, mobile-first field reference for learning wild plants and fungi through identification, food, nutrition, documented traditional knowledge, history, modern research and safety.

START HERE

Foraging safety

Never eat, drink, or medicinally use a wild plant or fungus based solely on this app. Positive identification requires multiple diagnostic features, and edible organisms can have dangerous or deadly look-alikes.

Every dossier now follows the same evidence rules. Traditional or historical use does not establish clinical effectiveness, missing nutrition data is not guessed, and herbs can interact with medicines.

RECIPROCITY

Stewardship, not extraction

Harvest only where permitted. Take only what you need. Protect populations and habitat, leave enough for wildlife and regeneration, and avoid creating waste while gathering or preparing food and medicine.

Urban Forager helps you learn what the land can provide. Zerra continues that relationship by helping reduce waste and build more sustainable everyday habits.

HOW CLAIMS ARE HANDLED

Accuracy & evidence policy

Urban Forager applies one evidence standard across all 174 current dossiers and future additions. Claims are retained only when supported, explicitly qualified, or clearly labeled as historical/traditional documentation.

  • Identification: verify names, diagnostic features, range and look-alikes against botanical authorities and qualified field references.
  • Nutrition: prefer quantitative food-composition data; unsupported qualitative rankings are removed rather than treated as verified.
  • Medicine: distinguish traditional use, preclinical research, human evidence and safety.
  • Indigenous uses: identify the specific nation/community and historical source rather than collapsing distinct peoples into a generic claim.
  • Uncertainty: gaps or conflicting evidence are stated rather than filled in.
